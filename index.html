THE CHIP

Firmware that looks after itself.

  • ESP32-S3 firmware in C on FreeRTOS: sensor data over UART, BLE, deep-sleep wake on motion.
  • Fault detection with automatic recovery, and over-the-air updates. Each avoided site visit saves about half a day.
  • I also wrote preflash, which finds the boot, encryption and OTA settings firmware ships with switched off.
THE PIN

Where it starts.

Every system I secure starts here: a signal on one pin of one chip.

THE BOARD

Power, on its own.

  • Carrier PCB in KiCad, brought up with a scope and a multimeter.
  • Battery power: cell pack, BMS, buck-boost and a current-limited load switch. No wiring run to the door.
THE DOOR

Where the real world disagrees.

  • Enclosures in FreeCAD, mounted at real doors next to the reader, lock and panel.
  • On go-live day the electric lock was corrupting the sensor link. I traced it on site and redesigned the link the same day; the launch went ahead on time.
THE BUILDING

Every device, known.

  • An access-control system (card and face readers, electric locks, control panels) live at a commercial site.
  • My own MDM for about 40 devices, patched on a fixed schedule. RHEL inside Active Directory, hardened and then attacked.
INSIDE · THE NETWORK

What's kept apart.

INSIDE · THE POLICY
